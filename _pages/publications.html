Development Policy Evaluation

- Dingwei Guo. “Unburdened Fields, Mobilized Workers: Labor Market Responses to Agricultural Tax Reform and Intergenerational Consequences.” Job Market Paper

Abstract

In many developing economies, the coexistence of low productivity agriculture and surplus labor in farming reflects a fundamental sectoral misallocation that slows structural transformation and limits long run growth. This paper studies how the agricultural tax reform in China between 2000 and 2006 reshaped this distorted equilibrium. First, by employing a staggard difference-in-differences (DID) design, I document a large and immediate financial benefits from tax reform on rural families. I argue that rural households responded by investing heavily in agricultural machinery, which substituted for manual labor and reduced labor input in farm sectors. These adjustments facilitated labor reallocation into higher-productivity non-farm employment. Second, I employ a cohort DID specification to study intergenerational impacts. I show the exposure to tax reform at school ages significantly raised the educational attainments of teenagers and generated more long-run benefits in their adulthood, suggesting a persistent gain in human capital accumulation and labor allocation adjustment. Finally, I show these micro-level changes are different between genders and are concentrated on females and girls. The results highlight how fiscal reforms that relax production constraints can trigger technology adoption, mitigate labor misallocation, and generate persistent improvements in human capital formation.

- Dingwei Guo, Yuan Zhao, and Kaixi He. “Beyond the Farm: How Agricultural Cooperatives Reshape Rural Labor Allocation and Income.” Under review.

Abstract

This paper studies how agricultural cooperatives reshape rural households’ labor allocation and income composition by improving farm marketing. Using a rich household-level panel data from a county from 2013 to 2019, we exploit staggered adoption of cooperative membership and implement a difference-in-differences design to identify causal effects. We find that joining a cooperative increases both farm and off-farm income by 19 percent and 22 percent, respectively, indicating that cooperatives complement rather than crowd out off-farm activities. On the farm side, cooperative participation raises agricultural profits without increasing production expenditures, consistent with improvements in marketing efficiency and stronger bargaining power in output markets. These gains are larger for households in remote and poorly connected areas and in villages with higher cooperative participation intensity. On the off-farm side, cooperative members increase both the duration of off-farm work and off-farm wage rates, reflecting adjustments along both the quantity and quality margins of employment. We further document that cooperative development is associated with greater clustering of off-farm employment destinations within villages, consistent with network-based information diffusion and improved job matching. The results highlight the broader role of agricultural institutions in alleviating market and information frictions and in accelerating rural structural transformation.

- Dingwei Guo, Yuan Zhao, and Xiyan Li. “Leave or Stay: Inequality of the Poverty Alleviation Resettlement Program.” Journal of Development Effectiveness (2026): 1–25. [Journal] [Unedited Version] [Appendix]

Abstract

This paper examines how poverty alleviation resettlement affects household labour allocation and income. We identify two competing mechanisms: a last-mile channel that lowers migration costs and promotes urban work, and a home-attachment channel that improves local amenities and discourages migration. Using panel data and a difference-in-differences design, we find that resettlement raises total income and migrant wages by shifting labour from farming to urban employment. However, gains are concentrated among labour-abundant households, while labour-constrained households experience stagnant or declining earnings. These findings show that resettlement can unintentionally widen inequality, underscoring the role of household labour endowments in shaping programme impacts.

- Hanchen Li, Yuan Zhao, Ruting Huang, Xiyan Li, and Dingwei Guo. “Give a Man a Fish? Passive Income versus Livelihood Support for Persistent Poverty Alleviation.” Working in progress.

Abstract

This paper examines whether poverty alleviation programs that strengthen households’ livelihood capacity generate more persistent poverty reduction than programs that primarily provide income support. We study China’s Industrial Poverty Alleviation Programs (IPAP) using household-level administrative panel data from 2014 to 2020 and exploit variation in the timing of program participation in a difference-in-differences framework. We measure persistent poverty risk using household vulnerability to expected poverty. On average, participation in IPAP significantly reduces household vulnerability while increasing income and productive engagement. However, these effects differ substantially across program types. Production-oriented programs increase production income and productive investment, while employment-oriented programs shift household labor toward wage employment; both reduce vulnerability to poverty. In contrast, photovoltaic programs substantially increase household income through asset-based dividends but generate little change in productive or employment activities and are associated with higher vulnerability. The benefits of livelihood-oriented programs are also larger among households with stronger labor and human-capital endowments. These findings suggest that raising current income alone may be insufficient for persistent poverty reduction; interventions that strengthen households’ own income-generating capacity can provide more durable protection against future poverty.

- Jie Feng, Yuan Zhao, Kaixi He, Dingwei Guo, and Zhengrun Chen. “Beyond Targets: Spatial Spillovers of Targeted Poor County Program.” Working in progress.

Abstract

This paper examines the spatial spillover effects of China’s 2011 Targeted Poor County (TPC) program, which directs central government fiscal transfers to designated poor counties for investments in infrastructure, industrial development, and worker training. We extend a difference-in-differences framework to distinguish direct effects in target counties from spillover effects in adjacent non-target counties. Using household-level data from the National Fixed Point Survey and county-level statistical data, we find that the program increased household income and induced substantial rural labor reallocation from agricultural to nonfarm activities in both target and adjacent non-target counties. We further document three channels underlying these effects: improved transportation facilitating cross-county worker mobility, industrial expansion generating local agglomeration spillovers, and greater access to agricultural machinery services reducing agricultural labor demand. These findings show that place-based development policies can generate economically meaningful effects beyond target administrative boundaries.

Non-Market Good Valuation, Survey Methodology, and Experimentation

- Yuan Zhao and Dingwei Guo. "When Money Crowds Out Morals: A Study of Demand-Side Management Participation." Under review.

Abstract

This study investigates how extrinsic incentives interact with intrinsic prosocial motivation in shaping household participation in Direct Load Control (DLC) programs. Building on a theoretical framework of motivation crowding-out, we combine two complementary experiments to examine whether and how extrinsic incentives affect prosocial participation. The first experiment uses a discrete choice experiment (DCE) to elicit respondents’ preferences for key DLC attributes and provides suggestive evidence of a U-shaped relationship between compensation and DLC acceptance. Motivated by these findings and to address potential identification concerns inherent in the DCE design, the second experiment uses a randomized design that varies monetary incentives. We find that prosocial motivation encourages DLC participation, while small monetary incentives can erode this motivation and reduce participation rates. However, sufficiently large monetary incentives can reverse this effect by dominating the decision-making process. These results underscore the importance of carefully balancing intrinsic motivation and extrinsic incentives in the design of demand-side management (DSM) programs.

- Dingwei Guo, Yuan Zhao, and Zhi Li. "Consistency across WTP Elicitation Formats under Valuation Uncertainty: Evidence from a Large-Scale Water Diversion Project in China." Under review. [Latest Version]

Abstract

This paper examines whether alternative cmontingent valuation (CV) formats elicit consistent willingness to pay (WTP) when respondents face valuation uncertainty. Using a survey experiment conducted on the Han-to-Wei River Water Diversion Project in Shaanxi, China, we randomly assign 1,218 respondents to double-bounded dichotomous choice (DBDC) or multiple-bounded discrete choice (MBDC) questionnaires, with MBDC allowing respondents to express valuation uncertainty directly. We find that DBDC estimates of WTP converge around the valuation region associated with MBDC Not sure responses when evaluated over a common bid range. Combined with the consistency between single-bounded dichotomous choice (SBDC) and MBDC Not-sure responses documented in the literature, our result supports the conjecture that a Not-sure response is more likely to be observed when a bid is closer to the mean of one' s value distribution implied by Wang (1997). These results indicate that standard CV formats provide comparable welfare measures, supporting cross-format validation in stated-preference research.

- Yuan Zhao, Weihua Yu, Dingwei Guo, and Xiaoping He. “Household Customers’ Cost of Interruption: Survey Study on Summer Electricity Peak of Xi’an City.” Frontiers in Energy Research 9 (2022): 802018. [Journal]

Abstract

In light of China’s Carbon Neutrality Target and facing the fluctuating pressure of power supply brought on by new energy intermittent power generation, it is urgent to mobilize a large number of residential flexible loads that can respond instantaneously to mitigate peak–valley difference. Under a framework of demand-side management (DSM) and utility analysis, we empirically investigate customers’ costs from interrupting typical electrical terminals at the household level. Specifically, by using the contingent valuation method (CVM), we explore the factors that affect households’ Willingness to Accept (WTA) of voluntarily participating in the interruption management during the summer electricity peak and estimate the distribution of households’ WTA values. We find that given the value of WTA, households’ participation rate in the interruption management significantly decreases with the increase in interruption duration and varies with the type of terminal appliance that is on direct interruption management. Moreover, the majority of households are willing to participate in the interruption management even if the compensation amount is low. The factors that determine households’ WTA and the size of their influences vary with the type of electrical terminal. The results imply that differentiating the terminal electricity market and accurately locking on the target terminals by considering the household heterogeneity can reduce the household welfare losses arising from DSM.

Intra-Household Decision

- Zining Li, Yuan Zhao, and Dingwei Guo. "Who Earns, Who Bears: Relative Income, Bargaining Power, and Fertility Decisions." R&R. [Latest Version]

Abstract

This study investigates how the distribution of income between husbands and wives is associated with fertility decisions. Using panel data from China Family Panel Studies (CFPS), we analyze how absolute and relative spousal incomes are related to the number of children in a household. Results show that higher husband income is positively associated with fertility, while higher wife income and a larger share of wife income are negatively associated with fertility. These asymmetric relationships reflect the unequal costs of childbearing borne primarily by women, where higher female earnings amplify the opportunity cost of motherhood and may strengthen bargaining power within the household. We provide evidence consistent with this mechanism by using spouses’ relative time contribution in daily housework as a proxy for intra-household bargaining power and showing that women with higher relative income spend less time on household chores, suggesting greater decision-making power that is associated with lower fertility. Heterogeneity analyses reveal that the negative association between women’s income and fertility is stronger among low-income and rural households. The findings highlight the importance of policies aimed at reducing women’s childbearing costs, directing fertility subsidies specifically to women, and prioritizing support for low-income and rural families to achieve sustainable fertility outcomes.
